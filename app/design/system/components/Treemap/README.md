The treemap on its own: the app's canvas/WebGL painter and its layout, on the sample drive.

Give it a sized slot (it fills it). Use it to try the map's look without the window around it: `pad` (inset inside open folders, classic look 3), `gap` (between tiles, app 2), `header` (folder title band, classic look 21), `minHeaderW` (60), `minArea` (110), `minSide` (24), `minWidth` (44), `labelMinW` (44), `labelMinH` (20), `radius` (tiles, 3) and `folderRadius` (open folders, 4) all apply live. It also takes `scenario`, `scheme`, `lang` and `colorBy` (`type`/`age`).

The folder look is `folderLook="band"`, as the app ships it since 1.3.1: every folder is one frame, its title band's fill runs down into the white area's corners (and, with `ring`, on round the sides and bottom), content sits `white` px (4) from the white area on all four sides, and a closed folder shows its colour as a `foot` px (5) band along the bottom, `tone` (1) steps lighter. With `fitSize="on"` (the app) each folder's least width follows its own size label on one line (label width + 16, never under `minWidth`) and its least height is 45; a folder too small for that joins the small items. With `fitSize="off"`, `minWhite` (6) is the least white area a folder gets and the folder minimum height follows from it. With the band look, `pad` and `header` follow from these and are ignored. `folderLook="classic"` draws the look up to 1.3.0, for comparison; there `pad` and `header` apply.

Its tiles are drawn on a canvas, so they cannot be selected or restyled one by one in an editor: change the props, or change `app/src/lib/treemap` in the app. Hover and selection never change a tile's geometry.
